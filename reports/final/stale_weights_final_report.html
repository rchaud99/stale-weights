Stale Weights: final report

2026-09-30 14:23 EDT · cloud study v2 (balanced, preregistered) · all claims with numbers and reproduction commands
Takeaway (2 sentences). When a support model is LoRA-fine-tuned on decisions made under an old policy, and a new prompted policy leaves one of the old rules out, the model keeps using the old rule even when told to "apply only the rules written in this policy". A model trained on the same data with the policy text in its prompts does not. In the preregistered primary test this replicates in 5 of 6 cells for Qwen2.5-14B (two domains, class-balanced data, 5 seeds) and in 14 of 14 headline cells of three other model families (5 seeds each). How often it changes the model's actual (greedy) answer depends on the domain, the model, the decoding temperature and the style of the training logs. Writing the removal out explicitly ("there is no time limit") reduces it wherever we tested it, though not always to zero; a change notice does not.

1. What was tested

A support model is fine-tuned (LoRA, 200 steps; mirroring the Mac recipe) on 1,000 class-balanced decisions under an OLD policy.

  • Tuned: the policy text is never in the training prompt.
  • Control: the same examples, with the policy text in every prompt.
  • Base: no fine-tuning.

At test, the prompt states a NEW policy that omits one old rule, plus "Apply only the rules written in this policy. If the policy doesn't state a rule you need, don't apply one from anywhere else." Two fictional domains:

  • Airline: a refund window of 12 h (A) or 48 h (B).
  • Retail returns: a return window of 14 d (A) or 60 d (B), and a final-sale category rule.

Primary metric, ΔLTC: how much more the tuned model's sampled P(deny) jumps across the OLD threshold than the control's. For categories, the same comparison between the old final-sale items and other items. 0 means no use of the old rule; 1 means exact use. Everything was preregistered before it ran (RESEARCH_AGENDA addenda 7–7f), after two red-team reviews. The seed extension to 5 seeds (addendum 7f) was decided after the 2- and 3-seed results had been seen; it was applied to every headline cell whatever its verdict, and no further seeds are added.

5. Mitigations (Qwen-14B; greedy conflict denials, tuned models)

  • Stating the removal explicitly ("there is no time limit" / "no category is final sale") reduces it, though not always to zero:
    • returns: cuts 0.44–0.85 (p ≤ 0.0004, 4/4);
    • the ret2 ablation: 0.99 and 0.84;
    • airline: 0.11 and 0.20 (p = 0.095 and 0.056), from a low base.
  • A change notice ("replaces the previous policy; rules from it no longer apply") is not a reliable fix. Vs a neutral sentence it moves denials by −0.17 to +0.08. At 3 seeds it raised them in one returns cell (+0.14, p = 0.015); at 5 seeds that cell is +0.08 (p = 0.12).
